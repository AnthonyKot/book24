# Chapter 4, pair form: stay or go, beginner adaptation (received 2026-09-27)

Supplied by the reader from another agent's answer; filed as the candidate one-teammate practice for chapter 4. Not yet in a chapter. Assessment at the end.

Claimed basis: FIFA "in and out of possession" (`sources/fifa-in-out.md`) for stay/go decisions and for distinguishing collection, smothering and shot responses. The source note supports stay/go, the distance-dependent postures and the named actions (smother, block, spread); "adjusting to the attacker's touch" is not in the source note as fetched and stays the book's own until the session-plan PDF is read.

Setup: one teammate, one ball, two cones, a goal, even grass. Walking pace to start. Agreed rule: the teammate stops challenging when the keeper moves to collect; no contested slides, no kicks at hands.

| Stage | Time | What to do | What counts as good |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prepare | 4 min | Comfortable movement, short sideways steps, gentle catches, ground-ball collections | Moving and collecting without rushing or discomfort |
| Follow the attacker | 4 min | Teammate dribbles slowly at the keeper, changes direction; no shots, no tackles; reset before getting crowded | On the feet and able to follow the next touch |
| Read two different touches | 5 min | Teammate alternates close control with deliberately releasing the ball towards the keeper, then stops; keeper says "hold" or "collect" as they decide | Close control does not trigger a slide; the released ball is collected without a race |
| Add a shot option | 5 min | From a comfortable distance the teammate either shoots gently or keeps dribbling; keeper stays mobile through the dribble, ready for the shot; reset before close contact | Responding to what happens, not falling before the action |
| Review | 2 min | Two repetitions discussed: one deliberate choice, one automatic reaction | The keeper can name the cue used |

Smothering: practise ground-ball collection with no attacker first. If the landing and collection technique has not been learned, keep to standing collections and have a goalkeeper coach demonstrate the grounded version before adding it. "Hands-first" alone is not instruction.

Progression: once the choices are comfortable, the teammate mixes them unannounced. Increase uncertainty before speed.

## Assessment (book's own, 2026-09-27)

Fits the reader: twenty minutes, one teammate, no jumps or dives, standing collections first (CONTEXT §8), and the agreed no-contest rule addresses the reflex slide directly (CONTEXT §7). The "hold" / "collect" call is the mechanism chapter 4 needs: it forces the decision to be made aloud before the body makes it.

What the chapter must add before this becomes its practice:

1. **The cue.** "Deliberately releasing the ball" is the teammate's instruction, not the keeper's cue. The keeper's cue has to be visible from the goal: the ball more than a stride from the attacker's foot, with the keeper closer to it than the attacker's next touch. Stage 3 needs that sentence, and the chapter's decision is built on it.
2. **Distances.** "Comfortable distance" in stage 4 is unmeasured on purpose; the chapter should still give the reader a way to set it (start at the penalty spot, 11 m, and shorten by a stride each week the stage feels easy), because `fifa-in-out` puts the smother inside about 3 m and the tall posture outside it.
3. **A solo form and a squad form** (BRIEF). Solo: the same stages against a wall are not possible; a solo form is footwork and standing collections only, with the decision practised by watching, not doing. Squad: the FIFA 2v2 game (part 1) is the squad form, once the pair form is comfortable.
4. **The keeper-coach dependency.** The session defers the grounded smother to a coach the reader does not have. The chapter either finds a verified beginner progression for landing and collection (`sources/england-defend-the-goal-notebooklm.md` "evidence still needed", item 4) or keeps the whole practice standing, and says so.
5. **What "good" means for the slide.** The session removes slides from practice, rightly, but the chapter has to say what replaces the slide in a match when the cue is absent: stay on the feet, narrow the angle at walking pace, keep the shot available. That is the decision's "what goes wrong on either side".
