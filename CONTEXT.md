## 4. Voice

- Second person for the reader. "You will see the striker's head drop before the shot."
- One situation at a time, described as it happens on the pitch, from the keeper's eyes.
- Numbers only where the reader can check them on a pitch (distances, counts of touches, seconds); no statistics from memory.
- Name the failure mode inside the advice, not in a footnote: what goes wrong if you advance too early is part of the sentence that tells you when to advance.
- No hero keepers, no anecdotes about professionals unless a source's session uses one; the point is the decision, not the person.
- No war-metaphor register. No selling. Nothing anthropomorphised; a defence does not "want", it is positioned or it is not.
- A chapter ends on the observation to review, never on a slogan.
